This page provides an illustration for the Domestic Revenue Mobilization course, focusing on the issue of aggressive tax planning and selected policy responses. The simulation component allows users to assess the impact of different tax optimization strategies on both group profitability and government revenue. It also incorporates simulations of selected countermeasures.

## Model notes and limitations

The interactive simulation is a stylised teaching tool, not a calibrated forecast or a legal assessment. Monetary figures are in USD millions. The reference case assumes turnover of 300, purchases of 50, wages of 100, annual depreciation of 20, interest of 40, and a 5% turnover royalty. With a 20% corporate income tax, the reference yields 75 in taxable profit, 15 in CIT and 15 in royalty (30 in total host-country revenue).

The CEO and Minister views represent separate illustrative exercises rather than a simultaneous strategic equilibrium. The Minister exercise starts from an assumed aggressive-planning case. Offshore profit and transfer-pricing adjustments are schematic and should not be interpreted as consolidated accounting statements. In particular, equipment transfer values are capital stocks, while depreciation and taxes are annual flows: cross-period comparisons require caution.

### Quality checks

- With all CEO strategies off and default parameters: CIT = 15, royalty = 15, shareholder return = 60.
- Turning on thin capitalisation while transfer pricing is active must preserve the equipment-financing component of interest expenses.
- Non-finite, unknown, or out-of-bounds slider values must not enter the simulation state.
- The arm's-length correction may reduce, but must never increase, an inflated invoice value.
